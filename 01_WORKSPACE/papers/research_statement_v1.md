## 1. Research interests

I want to do research on **calibrated machine-learning methodology for scientific domains where ground truth is fundamentally unknowable** — situations where the only honest output of a model is an inference with an error bar, never a verified detection. My interest lives at the intersection of three methodological lines I have practised in code, not only on paper: (i) **positive-unlabelled (PU) learning** for tasks where confirmed negatives are unavailable; (ii) **conformal and Bayesian calibration** so every reported confidence is a coverage statement, not a probability number; and (iii) **scale-bridging multi-modal fusion**, in which data of four orders of magnitude in spatial resolution (point samples, orbital rasters, regional mosaics, gravity fields) must be combined without one modality silently overwriting another.

The throughline is methodological maturity under epistemic uncertainty. In many scientific domains — exoplanet characterisation, subsurface planetary voids, drug-target binding, dark-matter subhalos — the positive class is rare, the unlabelled set is contaminated, and the only honest question is *how strongly the available evidence favours a candidate*. I am interested in formalising this question: turning a registry of inferences into a tiered, FP-bounded output that survives adversarial audit. The LUNARVOID project I describe below is my concrete test-bed for these ideas; the research direction itself is broader.

## 2. Current project portfolio

LUNARVOID is a 62-session solo research program that builds a calibrated, multi-evidence framework for **inferring candidate lunar lava-tube voids from orbital morphometry and geophysics** — never detecting them. The project's central thesis, which I treat as a methodological commitment and not a slogan, is that FP per 10⁴ km² (calibrated against a context-specific held-out set, Wilson 95% CI) is the only legitimate unit of scientific reporting in this problem space. Headline frozen state at the repository (`01_WORKSPACE/notes/RESUME.md`): a 278-row candidate registry (md5-pinned at `a60fb52152e3`), FP = **3.74 [1.71, 7.10] per 10⁴ km²** over 24,063 km² of searched mare, with full audit-trail from the on-disk evidence (`01_WORKSPACE/admin/CHANGELOG.md`).

The portfolio comprises four shipped, auditable artefacts:

1. **The framework itself.** A PU-learning baseline plus a fusion prototype that consumes LROC NAC DTMs, Diviner thermal bands, and GRAIL gravity gradients, producing tiered candidate rows. Every reported number traces to a CSV or JSON under `01_WORKSPACE/data/outputs/` and is reproducible byte-identically (test suite 124 passed; v02/v03/v04 verifier scripts each PASS 21/21, 15/15, 11/11 respectively).
2. **WP0.5 — the roof-deformation forward model** (`01_WORKSPACE/code/wp0_5_deflection/`). A three-regime analytical forward model — clamped slab, clamped arch, cumulative damage — whose 2,880-row parameter sweep (`sweep_results.csv`) ties roof-sag amplitude to NAC DTM detection floors. The headline finding is that intact elastic flexure of competent basalt is **always** below the 4 m detection floor by 3–5 orders of magnitude; only the near-collapse regime produces a detectable signal, and only one candidate site (Marius Hills) sits plausibly close to that threshold. This reframes the targeting criterion: instead of asking "is there a pit here?", the model now asks "is the regime here one in which detection is physically plausible?". This is a calibration-aware forward model for sparse-label scientific inference, and I believe it is publishable as a methodology contribution in its own right.
3. **LLTB-1 — the Lunar Lava-Tube Benchmark 1** (`01_WORKSPACE/notes/2026-08-22_LLTB1_v0.5_release_note.md`). A reproducible benchmark of my detector chain on a terrestrial analog (Indian Tunnel, Lava Beds National Monument). Byte-identical reproduction across machines; full ladder of detector F1 against a frozen ground-truth mask; seven sites. Best honest F1 0.362 at the cliff edge. Recall = 1.00 at every rung with ≥5 void cells; the bottleneck is precision, not detection power.
4. **The LUNARVOID Portal** (`luna-web/`, React 19 + Vite 8 + React-Three-Fiber). A five-tab public research surface that renders the registry, the analog benchmark, the forward model, and the calibration thesis on a photorealistic PBR moon. Phase 4 hardened (67 tests, lint/typecheck/format/build all 0); not yet pushed to GitHub Pages — this is on the deployment queue.

What did not work is documented, not hidden. NAC stereo-reproduction on local hardware gave a 4–9 m standard deviation against the published 0.72 m spec — a geometric limit at 0.5° convergence, not a software bug (`2026-09-04_AUDIT_REVIEW.md`). The Pit Atlas attribute table misses two of eight pits (positional join is the source of truth). The first D3 LOO cross-validation was deliberately frozen because reopening it would move published numbers; that is queued as user-gated work.

## 3. Specific PhD topic proposal

For the PhD I want to pursue **PU learning + conformal calibration as a unified methodology for scientific discovery with sparse, contaminated labels**, using the LUNARVOID forward model and fusion stack as the test-bed. The specific question is: *given a forward model that quantifies which regimes are physically detectable, can we build a conformal wrapper around a PU-trained classifier that produces tiered inferences with a coverage guarantee at every tier, and validate this wrapper on a held-out physical-completeness test rather than only on i.i.d. labels?*

## 4. Why a CSE student is doing planetary work, and why a CSE/AI PhD fits

A planetary case study is the test-bed where these methods are forced to be honest. In CS benchmarks (CIFAR, GLUE, ImageNet) we have i.i.d. labels and the question is accuracy. In planetary science we have *one Moon*, *no labelled negatives*, and a noise model that is not stationarity. A CSE skillset — algorithms, statistical methodology, software engineering, claim discipline — is precisely what this domain is missing. My contributions live in ML methodology (the forward model, the calibration wrapper, the PU baseline, the fusion prototype), not in planetary domain knowledge. A CSE/AI PhD is therefore the right training: it gives me a methodological home where these contributions are evaluated as method contributions, not as service to a domain.
