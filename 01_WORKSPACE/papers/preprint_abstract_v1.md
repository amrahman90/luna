## Abstract

**We do not detect lunar lava tubes; we infer them, with error bars.** A central but previously unaddressed question for any morphometric candidate is whether the *physically expected* surface expression of a subsurface void lies above or below the orbital detection floor. We present an analytical three-regime forward model of roof deformation — intact clamped slab (δ = ρgL⁴ / 32Eh²), intact clamped arch (R/L = 0.2 reduction), and a cumulative-damage regime in which Young's modulus and effective thickness scale with a damage factor — with a 2,880-row parameter sweep tying elastic flexure to NAC DTM detection floors across plausible lunar roof geometries. The model is verified at seven anchor points within ±5% of an external F1 canonical table (max 1.10% at ρ = 3000 kg/m³). The headline finding: **intact elastic flexure of competent basalt is below the 4 m detection floor by three to five orders of magnitude** across the full physical range; only the near-collapse regime (damage factor ≥ 0.6, span L ≥ 300 m) produces a detectable signal. Of the catalogued pit sites, Mare Tranquillitatis (the only site with independent radar evidence) is undetectable by elastic flexure of an intact roof; Mare Ingenii is intermediate; Marius Hills is closest to the threshold but only under a wider-rille-segment interpretation. The methodological contribution is a *targeting criterion*: the forward model tells us which physical regimes are detectable, and therefore where scanning is informative — turning an FP-bounded inference problem into one with a physics-informed prior. The sweep, four-panel figure, verifier, and data are released open-source (CC-BY-4.0 / PDS public domain); a Zenodo deposit is staged and a 124-test reproducibility suite passes byte-identically. We offer the model as a calibration-aware template for sparse-label scientific inference where the detection floor is set by instrument noise, not model uncertainty.
