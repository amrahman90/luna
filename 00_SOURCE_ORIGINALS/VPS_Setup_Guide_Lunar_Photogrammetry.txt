================================================================================
RUNNING THE LUNAR LAVA TUBE PIPELINE ON A RENTED VPS
Practical sizing, cost, data sourcing and setup guide
Prepared: 31 July 2026
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
0. SHORT ANSWER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

YES. All software is free and open source, all data is public and free, and it
all runs on Linux. No licences, no institutional access required.

BUT: a standard cheap VPS (2 vCPU / 4 GB RAM / 80 GB SSD) will fail. Not
"be slow" - fail. Ames Stereo Pipeline will run out of memory and die.

THE KEY INSIGHT that saves you most of the cost:

  *** You do not need to run photogrammetry at all for the first phase. ***

  Hundreds of LROC NAC Digital Terrain Models are ALREADY PROCESSED and
  published as PDS Reduced Data Record products, downloadable as GeoTIFF,
  along with a footprint shapefile containing per-DTM error metrics. The LROC
  team releases new ones quarterly.

  Your Gap-2 roof-sag detector, your depression-depth rasters, your terrain
  derivatives, your feature stacking, your PU-learning classifier - ALL of that
  runs on these pre-made DTMs. On a $10/month box.

  Only build your own DTMs later, when you need coverage that does not exist
  yet, or when you need SfS refinement beyond the published product.

So sequence it: cheap box first, big box only when justified.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THREE TIERS OF INFRASTRUCTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

TIER 0 - "ANALYSIS ONLY"                    approx. $6-20 / month
  Spec:      2-4 vCPU, 8-16 GB RAM, 200-500 GB disk
  Runs:      GDAL, WhiteboxTools, RichDEM, SAGA, PDAL, Python/scikit-learn,
             pyshtools, QGIS headless
  Does:      Published NAC DTM download and analysis, SLDEM2015/LOLA work,
             depression-depth rasters, anisotropic filtering, morphometrics,
             Diviner/Mini-RF/GRAIL raster fusion, classical ML, PU learning,
             candidate catalogue generation
  Covers:    Gaps 1 (partially), 2, 5, 6, 8 from the research plan
  Verdict:   THIS IS WHERE YOU START. Genuinely sufficient for a first paper.

TIER 1 - "PHOTOGRAMMETRY"                   approx. $50-150 / month
  Spec:      16-32 DEDICATED cores, 64-128 GB RAM, 1-2 TB NVMe
  Runs:      ISIS + Ames Stereo Pipeline, full NAC stereo DTM production
  Sizing evidence:
             ASP's own documentation gives a rule of thumb: images around
             20,000 x 20,000 px on a machine with 40 GB RAM and 16 cores can
             produce a terrain model in roughly 4-20 hours. An LROC NAC image
             is about 52,224 lines x 5,064 samples per CCD, so a stitched pair
             is in the same pixel-count ballpark. Budget the same or worse.
             SGM/MGM correlation subprocesses have been observed using over
             4 GB each; a common working config is 8 processes on a 64 GB node.
  Verdict:   Only rent this once you have a specific site that lacks a
             published DTM. Consider renting hourly/spot rather than monthly.

TIER 2 - "DEEP LEARNING"                    approx. $0.30-2.00 / hour, on demand
  Spec:      1 GPU with 16-24 GB VRAM, 8-16 vCPU, 64 GB RAM
  Runs:      PyTorch, PointNet++/KPConv/Point Transformer, Mask R-CNN baselines
  Verdict:   NEVER rent this monthly. Rent by the hour, or use free tiers
             (Google Colab, Kaggle) for prototyping and only pay for final runs.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. CRITICAL VPS BUYING TRAPS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

TRAP 1 - SHARED / BURSTABLE vCPU
  Budget VPS "vCPUs" are usually oversubscribed and throttled after a burst
  credit runs out. ASP will saturate every core for hours. You will get
  throttled to a crawl and be charged for the wall-clock time anyway.
  -> For Tier 1 you need DEDICATED vCPU instances, or a bare-metal / dedicated
     root server. Budget European dedicated-server providers give far better
     price-performance for this workload than mainstream cloud VPS.

TRAP 2 - DISK IS THE REAL BOTTLENECK
  ASP generates enormous intermediates: tiled disparity maps, subpixel
  refinement products, per-tile subdirectories. Expect scratch usage many times
  the input size. Budget 100-250 GB of scratch PER STEREO PAIR during
  processing, even though the final DTM is only a few GB.
  -> Use --keep-only to prune intermediates.
  -> NVMe, not spinning disk, not network-attached storage. ASP is I/O bound
     in several stages and NFS will destroy you.

TRAP 3 - BANDWIDTH CAPS AND EGRESS BILLING
  Many VPS plans cap monthly transfer. INBOUND is usually free or generous;
  OUTBOUND is what gets billed. You will download a lot and upload little, so
  this is usually fine - but check that inbound is genuinely unmetered.
  -> Never try to mirror the archive. Scale reference: the LROC team has
     delivered on the order of 579 TB of EDRs and over 1 PB of CDRs to PDS.
     Derived RDR products are a much smaller collection. Download only what
     you need, by ID.

TRAP 4 - THE ISIS DATA AREA
  The full ISIS ancillary data set for all missions is roughly 520 GB, most of
  it SPICE kernels. You do not need it.
  -> base area alone is around 26 GB.
  -> Fetch only the lro mission area, and exclude kernels.
  -> Use the SPICE Web Service instead of local kernels (see Section 4).
  -> If you later add Chandrayaan-2: its full ISIS directory is around 200 GB,
     almost entirely reconstructed attitude (ck) kernels covering the whole
     mission. Exclude them and pull only the two or three ck files spanning
     your acquisition dates via rclone.

TRAP 5 - OPERATING SYSTEM
  ISIS does not run on Windows natively. Any Linux VPS is fine. Ubuntu LTS or
  Rocky/Alma are the safest choices.

TRAP 6 - REBUILDING THE ENVIRONMENT
  Setting up ISIS + ASP + GDAL + a Python stack takes hours and the data
  download takes longer. Once it works, SNAPSHOT THE IMAGE. Then you can
  destroy and recreate large instances on demand and only pay while running.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. WHERE THE FREE DATA ACTUALLY IS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PRE-MADE DTMs (start here - no processing needed)
  LROC PDS RDR archive, NAC_DTM collection
      pds.lroc.asu.edu  and  lroc.im-ldi.com/data
      Delivered as PDS products plus GeoTIFF derivatives (colour-shaded relief,
      terrain-shaded relief, colour slope map) generated with GDAL.
  NAC DTM footprint SHAPEFILE
      Polygons for every published DTM with expanded error metrics, including
      relative linear error (vertical precision at 90% confidence) and
      triangulation RMS. Download this FIRST - it is your index and your
      quality filter in one file.
  NAC Stereo Catalog (PDF)
      Lists all NAC stereo pairs and triplets suitable for DTM generation.
      This tells you what you could build that does not exist yet.

GLOBAL BASE LAYERS
  SLDEM2015 (LOLA + Kaguya TC merged), ~59 m/px equatorial - USGS Astropedia
  LOLA gridded products - PDS Geosciences Node
  WAC global morphology mosaic and WAC DTM - LROC RDR

NON-OPTICAL EVIDENCE LAYERS
  Diviner thermal / rock abundance     - PDS Geosciences Node
  Mini-RF radar                        - PDS Geosciences Node
  GRAIL gravity models (GRGM1200A etc) - PDS Geosciences Node
  SELENE LRS radar sounder             - JAXA SELENE Data Archive
  M3 spectral                          - PDS Imaging Node

LABELS
  Lunar Pit Atlas (Wagner & Robinson) - ASU / LROC

RAW IMAGERY (only if building your own DTMs)
  LROC EDR/CDR via wms.lroc.asu.edu/lroc/search or the PDS Imaging Node
  Chandrayaan-2 OHRC / TMC-2 via ISRO ISSDC Pradan (free, requires
      registration; downloads arrive as zips with .img + .xml)

CLOUD-HOSTED MIRRORS
  Some planetary datasets are published on the AWS Registry of Open Data as
  Cloud Optimized GeoTIFFs, which allows windowed reads over HTTP without
  downloading whole files. Coverage is stronger for Mars/HiRISE than for the
  Moon at present - worth checking, because if a COG mirror exists for what you
  need, your storage requirement collapses to almost nothing.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. MINIMAL SETUP, TIER 1
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

# --- Miniforge + ISIS via conda -----------------------------------------
conda create -n isis -c usgs-astrogeology -c conda-forge isis rclone
conda activate isis
export ISISROOT=$CONDA_PREFIX
export ISISDATA=$HOME/isisdata
export ALESPICEROOT=$ISISDATA
mkdir -p $ISISDATA

# base area, ~26 GB
downloadIsisData base $ISISDATA

# LRO mission area WITHOUT the SPICE kernels
downloadIsisData lro $ISISDATA --exclude="kernels/**"

# --- Ames Stereo Pipeline (prebuilt binary, no compiling) ----------------
# grab the current release tarball from the ASP GitHub releases page
tar xjf StereoPipeline-*.tar.bz2
export PATH=$PWD/StereoPipeline-*/bin:$PATH

# --- NAC ingestion --------------------------------------------------------
f=M104318871LE
lronac2isis from=${f}.IMG to=${f}.cub
spiceinit   from=${f}.cub web=yes          # <-- uses SPICE Web Service,
                                           #     avoids 100s of GB of kernels
lronaccal   from=${f}.cub to=${f}.cal.cub radiometrictype=RADIANCE
lronacecho  from=${f}.cal.cub to=${f}.cal.echo.cub

  *** GOTCHA: lronaccal defaults to RadiometricType=IOF, which needs the
      target-to-sun distance. The SPICE Web Service does not attach that, so
      IOF against web=yes produces cubes full of zeros. Either use
      RADIANCE (as above), or download the local mission data. This one
      silently wastes days if you do not know it. ***

# --- stereo ---------------------------------------------------------------
bundle_adjust <cubes...> -o ba/run

parallel_stereo \
    --stereo-algorithm asp_mgm \
    --processes 8 \
    --threads-multiprocess 2 \
    --corr-memory-limit-mb 6000 \
    --bundle-adjust-prefix ba/run \
    <left.cub> <right.cub> stereo/run

point2dem --tr 5 --t_srs "<lunar equirectangular proj4>" stereo/run-PC.tif

  Tune --processes and --corr-memory-limit-mb together:
  processes x corr-memory-limit-mb must stay comfortably below total RAM.
  If a run dies mid-correlation, lower --processes and resume with
  --resume-at-corr rather than starting over.

# --- Tier 0 analysis stack ------------------------------------------------
conda create -n terra -c conda-forge \
    gdal rasterio rioxarray xarray whitebox richdem pdal python-pdal \
    geopandas scikit-learn xgboost pyshtools numpy scipy matplotlib

  Depression-depth raster (your CHM analog):
      whitebox_tools -r=FillDepressions -i=dtm.tif -o=filled.tif
      gdal_calc.py -A filled.tif -B dtm.tif --outfile=depth.tif --calc="A-B"

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. REALISTIC BUDGET AND SEQUENCING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

MONTHS 0-6   Tier 0 box only.
             Published DTMs + global layers + your own terrestrial drone data.
             Build the depression-depth detector, validate on Earth, run the
             degradation ladder. Produce paper 1.
             Cost: roughly $10-20/month plus storage.

MONTHS 6-12  Add Tier 2 GPU hours as needed, on demand only.
             Train the classifier. Do NOT keep a GPU idle.
             Cost: tens of dollars total if you prototype on free tiers.

MONTHS 12+   Spin up Tier 1 only when you have identified specific sites where
             no published DTM exists and the stereo catalog says a pair does.
             Process in batches, snapshot, destroy the instance between runs.
             Cost: hourly, bursty, likely a few hundred dollars total.

TOTAL over 18 months: plausibly under $600-800 if you sequence it this way.
If you rent a big permanent box from day one instead, the same 18 months costs
several thousand and most of those cores sit idle.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. CHEAPER OR FREE ALTERNATIVES WORTH CHECKING FIRST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - If you are affiliated with any university, ask about institutional HPC.
    ASP is already packaged as a module on several national HPC systems
    (with SLURM job scripts documented), so someone has done the install work.
  - Cloud provider research credit programmes (AWS, Google, Azure) routinely
    grant several thousand dollars of credit to academic and open-science
    projects. Worth an application before you spend your own money.
  - Google Colab / Kaggle free GPU tiers for all DL prototyping.
  - Spot / preemptible instances for photogrammetry: ASP's --resume-at-corr
    makes interrupted runs recoverable, which fits the spot model well.
  - Ask the LROC SOC directly whether a DTM exists for your target before
    building one. They release new NAC DTMs every quarter.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. WHAT WOULD ACTUALLY STOP YOU
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Not money. Not compute. Not data access. The realistic blockers are:

  1. Stereo coverage. Only a small fraction of the lunar surface has proper
     high-resolution stereo. No VPS fixes this.
  2. Scope discipline. Global processing is an HPC problem. Regional scope is
     a VPS problem. Choose regional.
  3. The ISIS/ASP learning curve, which is steep and poorly signposted. Budget
     4-6 weeks just to reproduce one published DTM end to end and confirm it
     matches within stated error bars. Treat that as a milestone, not a chore.

================================================================================
BOTTOM LINE

Rent a small box. Use the DTMs that already exist. Prove the method on your own
terrestrial drone data where you have ground truth. Only pay for cores once you
have a specific site that demands them. The expensive infrastructure is the
last thing you need, not the first.
