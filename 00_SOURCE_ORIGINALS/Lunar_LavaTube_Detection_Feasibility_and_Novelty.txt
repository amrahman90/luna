FROM DRONE FORESTRY POINT CLOUDS TO LUNAR LAVA TUBE DETECTION
Feasibility, Novelty and Research-Gap Analysis
Prepared: 31 July 2026
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
0. SHORT VERDICT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

FEASIBLE       : Yes, technically. Every stage of your drone pipeline has a
                 working lunar equivalent, and the data is free and public.

NOVEL          : Partially. The obvious version (CNN finds pits in orbital
                 images) is already published and crowded. The non-obvious
                 versions are wide open and genuinely publishable.

CRITICAL CAVEAT: The analogy breaks in one specific place, and if you do not
                 design around that break the project fails. See Section 2.

BEST FRAMING   : Not "detect lava tubes." Instead: "infer a calibrated,
                 uncertainty-quantified probability of subsurface void from
                 fused surface morphometry and geophysics, trained on
                 terrestrial analogs degraded to lunar observing conditions."

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. YOUR EXISTING PIPELINE, ABSTRACTED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Drone forestry / dead-tree detection, stripped to its logic:

  1. Overlapping imagery
  2. SfM-MVS -> sparse -> dense point cloud
  3. Ground classification (CSF / progressive TIN) -> DTM
  4. All-return surface -> DSM
  5. CHM = DSM - DTM   <- the key normalisation step
  6. Object segmentation (local maxima + watershed, or point-cloud clustering)
  7. Feature stack:
       structural : height, crown area, crown volume, verticality, roughness,
                    point density, gap fraction
       spectral   : NDVI, GNDVI, NDRE, VARI, GLI
       contextual : neighbourhood stats, slope, aspect, elevation
  8. Supervised classifier (RF / XGBoost / PointNet++) -> live / stressed / dead
  9. Validation against field survey

The transferable core is: NORMALISE TOPOGRAPHY AGAINST A LOCAL REFERENCE
SURFACE, SEGMENT ANOMALIES, STACK HETEROGENEOUS FEATURES, CLASSIFY, VALIDATE.
That core transfers to the Moon cleanly. The physics does not.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE CRITICAL BREAK IN THE ANALOGY  (read this before anything else)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A tree is ABOVE the surface. A lava tube is BELOW it.

Photogrammetry, on Earth or the Moon, reconstructs only the outer envelope of
the illuminated surface. A point cloud of the Moon contains exactly zero direct
information about a void 20-100 m beneath the regolith. You cannot "see" a lava
tube in a point cloud the way you see a dead crown in a CHM.

Everything you can do optically is INDIRECT INFERENCE from surface expression:

   DIRECT EVIDENCE (visible)
     - Pits / skylights: roof collapse holes. Rare but unambiguous.
     - Sinuous rilles: many are collapsed tubes, roof entirely gone.
     - Pit chains / collapse chains: aligned depressions along a lineament.

   INDIRECT EVIDENCE (subtle, mostly unexploited -> this is your opportunity)
     - Roof sag: a shallow, linear, laterally continuous residual depression
       above an intact tube, from slow flexure of the roof slab.
     - "Bridges": uncollapsed segments between two collapsed rille segments.
     - Anomalous regolith thermophysics above a thin roof.
     - Local mass deficit in gravity.

   SUBSURFACE EVIDENCE (true measurement, but coarse or sparse)
     - GRAIL Bouguer gravity gradients (mass deficit)
     - Radar sounding (SELENE LRS) and radar imaging (LRO Mini-RF)

Consequence for your design: DO NOT build a "detector." Build a multi-evidence
inference system where the point cloud is one input among several, and where
the output is a probability with an honest error bar. That reframing is also
where the novelty lives.

Corollary: negatives are not negatives. An area with no pit is not an area with
no tube. This makes it a positive-unlabeled (PU) learning problem, not a normal
binary classification problem. Most planetary ML papers get this wrong.

3. PARAMETER TRANSLATION TABLE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

DRONE / FORESTRY                 -> LUNAR EQUIVALENT
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RGB / multispectral imagery      -> LROC NAC 0.5-2.0 m/px
                                    Chandrayaan-2 OHRC ~0.25-0.30 m/px
                                    Kaguya/SELENE TC 10 m/px
                                    Chandrayaan-2 TMC-2 ~5 m/px
                                    Chang'E-2 CCD 7 m/px

SfM-MVS dense cloud              -> ASP parallel_stereo on NAC stereo pairs
                                    + point cloud fusion across multiple pairs
                                    + shape-from-shading (SfS) refinement

DTM (ground classification)      -> Regional trend / low-pass surface fitted to
                                    the NAC DTM; or SLDEM2015 (LOLA + Kaguya TC,
                                    ~59 m/px) as the "reference ground"

CHM = DSM - DTM                  -> RESIDUAL TOPOGRAPHY = DTM - trend surface.
                                    NOTE: inverted sign. You want NEGATIVE
                                    residuals (depressions), not positive.
                                    *** Practical trick: run a hydrological
                                    sink-fill (WhiteboxTools / RichDEM), then
                                    FILLED minus ORIGINAL. That difference
                                    raster is a depression-depth map and is the
                                    exact mathematical mirror of a CHM. This is
                                    the single cleanest bridge between your two
                                    domains. ***

Individual tree segmentation     -> Closed-depression segmentation; shadow-blob
                                    segmentation in raw NAC; connected-component
                                    labelling on the depression-depth raster

NDVI / GNDVI / NDRE              -> There is no true equivalent. Substitutes:
                                    - Diviner nighttime temperature anomaly
                                      (topography-removed, Powell et al. 2023)
                                    - Diviner rock abundance
                                    - Mini-RF circular polarisation ratio (CPR)
                                    - M3 / Kaguya MI derived FeO, TiO2
                                    - Optical maturity (OMAT)
                                    - Photometric roughness from multi-phase
                                      NAC observations

Tree height                      -> Pit depth (from shadow length + sun geometry,
                                    and from DTM)

Crown diameter                   -> Pit / depression diameter, funnel diameter

Crown volume                     -> Void volume estimate from 3D reconstruction

Verticality / point roughness    -> Wall slope, overhang presence, floor block
                                    abundance, curvature, surface roughness

Multi-TEMPORAL NDVI decline      -> Multi-ILLUMINATION stacking. The Moon is
                                    static, but sun azimuth/incidence varies
                                    across the >2.5 million NAC images. Treat
                                    illumination angle as the pseudo-temporal
                                    axis. Photometric stereo from that stack is
                                    a real and underused signal.

Field survey ground truth        -> Lunar Pit Atlas (Wagner & Robinson 2021),
                                    281 catalogued pits; Mini-RF conduit
                                    detection at Mare Tranquillitatis Pit
                                    (Carrer et al. 2024); LRS + GRAIL at
                                    Marius Hills (Kaku et al. 2017; Chappaz et
                                    al. 2017; Zhu et al. 2024)

No equivalent                    -> GRAIL gravity gradients (Gzz, Ghh) from
                                    GRGM1200A / GL1500E spherical harmonics.
                                    This is your only true volumetric sensor.

4. DATA FEASIBILITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT EXISTS AND IS FREE
  - LROC NAC EDR/CDR: >2.5 million images, most of the surface at ~0.7-1.5 m
  - LROC NAC stereo: historically only ~2% of the surface has proper
    high-resolution stereo coverage. This is the binding constraint.
  - Published LROC NAC DTMs: a few hundred sites, typically 2-5 m post spacing,
    registered to LOLA with sub-metre vertical / <10 m horizontal accuracy
  - SLDEM2015 (LOLA + Kaguya TC): global, ~59 m/px equatorial
  - LOLA: global altimetry, much denser near poles
  - Diviner: global thermal, ~128 ppd products with topographic removal
  - Mini-RF: S-band and X-band radar, partial coverage
  - GRAIL: global gravity to high degree/order, but effective spatial resolution
    only ~10-30 km
  - SELENE LRS: 5 MHz sounder, sparse orbital tracks
  - Chandrayaan-2 OHRC / TMC-2 via ISSDC Pradan; M3 via PDS Imaging
  - Lunar Pit Atlas (ASU) as the label set

FEASIBILITY BY STAGE
  Stereo DTM generation        : ROUTINE. ISIS + Ames Stereo Pipeline. Free,
                                 open source, well documented for NAC.
  SfS refinement               : FEASIBLE but delicate and compute-heavy;
                                 requires careful albedo/topography separation.
  Depression / residual maps   : TRIVIAL once you have DTMs.
  Feature stacking             : MODERATE. Reprojection and co-registration
                                 across instruments with wildly different
                                 footprints is the real work.
  ML classification            : FEASIBLE, but see Section 6 on label scarcity.
  Global-scale processing      : HARD. NAC images are ~52,000 x 5,000 px.
                                 Full-Moon processing is an HPC problem, not a
                                 laptop problem. Scope regionally.

HARD DATA LIMITS
  - Stereo coverage sparsity is the number one constraint. You cannot make a
    global high-resolution point cloud today.
  - Illumination: pit interiors are only lit near local noon at low latitudes.
    At high latitude and in polar regions, shadows destroy stereo matching.
  - Extreme class imbalance: ~281 catalogued pits across ~3.79 x 10^7 km^2 of
    lunar surface. And most of those are impact-melt pits, NOT lava-tube
    related. Only about 20 sit in mare or highland terrain, which is the
    subset actually relevant to tubes.
  - Ground truth is effectively nonexistent. One pit (Mare Tranquillitatis) has
    radar evidence of an actual conduit. Nothing has ever been measured in situ.

5. NOVELTY AUDIT - WHAT IS ALREADY PUBLISHED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Be honest with yourself about prior art before writing a proposal. The
following are DONE and will not carry a paper on their own:

  DONE - Automated pit detection by thresholding NAC images
         (PitScan; Wagner & Robinson 2014)
  DONE - Comprehensive pit catalogue
         (Lunar Pit Atlas, Wagner & Robinson 2021, 281 features)
  DONE - Deep learning detection of candidate cave entrances
         (Watson & Baldini, Icarus 2025). Mask R-CNN with ResNet50 backbone,
         trained jointly on lunar data, Martian HiRISE pit data, and synthetic
         lunar imagery made by implanting warped Martian pit pixels into empty
         NAC frames. Reported ~89% bbox F1 and ~96% mask F1.
  DONE - Photogrammetric 3D reconstruction of lava tube pits
         (Zhou et al. 2024, Earth and Space Science). Refined census cost,
         coarse-to-fine point cloud registration, weighted multi-pair fusion,
         2 m DTMs of Mare Tranquillitatis Pit and Marius Hills Hole.
  DONE - Mono/stereo 3D analysis of pit interiors (Wagner & Robinson 2022)
  DONE - GRAIL gradiometry and cross-correlation for buried void detection
         (Chappaz et al. 2017; Zhu et al. 2024)
  DONE - Radar sounder detection of subsurface echoes at Marius Hills
         (Kaku et al. 2017, SELENE LRS)
  DONE - Radar imaging evidence of an accessible conduit beneath the Mare
         Tranquillitatis Pit (Carrer et al. 2024, Nature Astronomy)
  DONE - Thermal characterisation of pits: Tranquillitatis and Ingenii pits run
         roughly 100 K warmer than surroundings at night; modelled cave
         interiors sit near a constant ~290 K
         (Horvath et al. 2022). Important negative result: thermal IR detects
         the PIT well but is essentially blind to the CAVE behind it.
  DONE - Roof stability and maximum stable width modelling
         (Blair et al. 2017; Theinat et al. 2020; Chwala et al. 2024)
  DONE - Terrestrial analog geophysics: ERT, GPR, TLS/LiDAR at Lanzarote
         Corona system, Lava Beds NM, Ape Cave; gravity forward modelling from
         a terrestrial analog (Montanez Munoz et al. 2025)
  DONE - Protolith / rock-competence context for the two flagship pits
         (Costello et al. 2026, JGR Planets): Tranquillitatis sits in competent
         dense basalt, Marius Hills in a more friable, vesicular protolith.

So: "I will use deep learning on orbital images to find lunar pits" is 2025
work. You need to go past it.

6. WHERE THE REAL NOVELTY IS  (ranked, highest first)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

GAP 1 - MULTI-MODAL, MULTI-SCALE PROBABILISTIC FUSION            [HIGH NOVELTY]
  Every evidence stream above exists as a SEPARATE paper. Nobody has built a
  single calibrated model that ingests NAC-DTM morphometry, Diviner thermal
  anomaly and rock abundance, Mini-RF CPR, GRAIL gravity gradients and LRS
  echoes, and outputs P(void | all evidence) with uncertainty.
  The hard and interesting part is the SCALE MISMATCH: your morphometry is at
  2-5 m and your gravity is at 10-30 km. That is 3-4 orders of magnitude. A
  hierarchical / multi-resolution Bayesian model, or a graph neural network
  over a multi-scale cell hierarchy, is a genuine methodological contribution
  in its own right and is publishable in a remote sensing venue independent of
  the lunar result.

GAP 2 - DETECTING UNCOLLAPSED TUBES FROM ROOF SAG                [HIGH NOVELTY]
  Everyone searches for holes. Almost nobody has systematically searched for
  the subtle, laterally continuous, linear residual depression that an INTACT
  roof over a void should produce. This is the closest true analog to your CHM
  workflow, and it is your strongest single idea.
  Method sketch: high-pass / trend-removed NAC DTM -> directional (anisotropic)
  filtering -> ridge/valley (Hessian eigenvalue, Frangi-style vesselness)
  detection tuned to linear depressions of 60-300 m width -> continuity and
  alignment scoring against mapped sinuous rilles -> screen candidates against
  mechanical plausibility (Gap 6).
  Risk to control: sinuous rilles, wrinkle ridges, graben, secondary crater
  chains and lava channels all produce linear depressions. Your entire
  contribution rests on discriminating sag from these. Build that
  discrimination test FIRST, on terrestrial data where you know the answer.

GAP 3 - TERRESTRIAL-TO-LUNAR DOMAIN ADAPTATION WITH PHYSICAL
        DEGRADATION AND A RESOLUTION-ABLATION STUDY          [HIGH-MED NOVELTY]
  This is where your existing drone expertise becomes an asset rather than a
  loose analogy.
  Published work has done Mars-to-Moon transfer and synthetic pit implantation.
  Nobody has taken real drone/LiDAR point clouds over terrestrial lava tube
  fields with KNOWN cave surveys underneath, and systematically degraded them
  to lunar observing conditions:
     - resample 2 cm -> 0.5 m -> 2 m -> 5 m -> 60 m
     - re-render under lunar photometric function (Hapke) at varied sun
       incidence/azimuth
     - inject NAC-like noise, MTF blur, and pushbroom geometry
     - remove atmospheric/vegetation returns to mimic bare regolith
  Then answer the question nobody has answered: AT WHAT GROUND SAMPLE DISTANCE
  AND SUN ANGLE DOES A TUBE SIGNATURE BECOME UNDETECTABLE? That single curve
  is a highly citable result and directly informs future mission camera specs.
  Analog sites with existing survey data: Corona/Lanzarote (~5 km TLS-mapped),
  Lava Beds NM California, Craters of the Moon Idaho, Undara Australia,
  Kazumura and Thurston Hawaii, Icelandic tubes, Ape Cave Washington.

GAP 4 - POINT-CLOUD-NATIVE DEEP LEARNING FOR PLANETARY SURFACES   [MED-HIGH]
  Essentially all planetary DL is 2D CNNs on rasters. Running PointNet++,
  KPConv or a Point Transformer directly on lunar point clouds - the way modern
  forestry does with PointNet++ on ALS data - is close to unexplored.
  Advantage: rotation/illumination invariance and native handling of overhangs,
  which a 2.5D raster literally cannot represent. Pit overhangs are exactly
  where rasters fail and point clouds win. Strong technical hook.

GAP 5 - POSITIVE-UNLABELED LEARNING AND CALIBRATED UNCERTAINTY         [MED]
  With ~20 relevant positives and no true negatives anywhere on the Moon,
  standard supervised metrics are close to meaningless. PU learning, one-class
  / anomaly detection (normalising flows, deep SVDD), conformal prediction for
  calibrated coverage, and explicit reporting of expected false positives per
  10^4 km^2 would be a methodological correction to the existing literature.
  This is a "we did the statistics properly" paper and those age well.

GAP 6 - PHYSICS-INFORMED SCREENING                                     [MED]
  Existing gravity-based candidates have produced geologically implausible
  geometries - one GRAIL analysis inferred a tube ~9 km wide, ~60 km long,
  buried ~605 m deep with ~55 m central height, which structural modellers
  regard as unlikely to be stable as a single void. Meanwhile finite-element
  limit analyses put realistic tube widths beneath observed skylights at
  roughly 60-300 m. Embedding those stability bounds as a prior or a
  regularisation term, so mechanically impossible candidates are automatically
  down-weighted, is a clean and defensible contribution.

GAP 7 - AUTOMATED, UNCERTAINTY-QUANTIFIED PIT INTERIOR RECONSTRUCTION  [MED]
  Extend Zhou et al. and Wagner & Robinson from hand-tuned single-site work
  into an automated pipeline: multi-illumination photometric stereo plus
  shadow-boundary constraints to reconstruct overhang geometry and the first
  few tens of metres of the void behind the pit wall, with per-vertex error
  bars. Directly feeds mission planning (descent corridors, anchor points).

GAP 8 - VALUE-OF-INFORMATION TARGET RANKING                            [MED]
  Given a ranked candidate list, which site should LunarLeaper-class or
  Moon-Diver-class missions actually visit to maximise expected information
  gain per euro? Combine your P(void) with accessibility (slope, boulder
  abundance, illumination and comms windows) and science return. This connects
  your work directly to live mission planning and makes it fundable.

LOW NOVELTY - AVOID AS A PRIMARY CONTRIBUTION
  - Plain CNN pit detection in NAC imagery
  - Re-cataloguing pits
  - Single-site photogrammetric reconstruction with no new method

7. CONCRETE PROJECT OPTIONS, SIZED
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

P1  MSc / 6-9 months  - "Resolution Limits of Lava Tube Detection"
    Drone survey a terrestrial tube field with a surveyed cave beneath.
    Build the point cloud. Degrade it through the lunar-conditions ladder.
    Measure detection performance vs GSD, sun angle and roof thickness.
    Deliverable: a detectability curve plus open code and dataset.
    Low risk, guaranteed result, immediately citable.

P2  MSc-PhD / 9-15 months - "Roof-Sag Signature Search"
    Implement the sink-fill-difference + anisotropic vesselness detector.
    Validate on terrestrial data (Gap 3 output), then run over all published
    LROC NAC DTMs in mare terrain. Produce a candidate catalogue with
    explicit confusion analysis against rilles, ridges and crater chains.
    Medium risk (the signal may be below noise), high reward if it works.

P3  PhD / 2-3 years - "Multi-Evidence Void Inference for the Moon"
    The full Gap 1 system. Hierarchical multi-scale fusion of NAC-DTM
    morphometry, Diviner, Mini-RF, GRAIL and LRS, with PU learning and
    conformal calibration, physics-informed screening, and a target-ranking
    layer. Regional scope: Marius Hills + Mare Tranquillitatis + Ingenii.
    Highest impact. Requires geophysics collaboration.

P4  Methods paper / 4-6 months - "Point Transformers for Planetary Terrain"
    Benchmark 2D CNN vs 3D point-cloud networks on the pit classification
    task, with explicit analysis of overhang cases where 2.5D rasters fail.
    Publishable in a remote sensing / ML venue on the method alone.

P5  Cross-body extension - do the same on Mars first.
    HiRISE gives 0.25 m/px with far better stereo coverage, CTX gives a
    global mosaic, and Martian pit catalogues are larger. If your goal is a
    METHODS contribution, Mars is the easier proving ground and the Moon
    becomes the transfer demonstration. This also strengthens Gap 3.

8. HONEST RISK REGISTER
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

R1  You cannot validate. Accept it and design for it: report calibrated
    probabilities, not "detections." Reviewers will forgive uncertainty; they
    will not forgive overclaiming.
R2  The roof-sag signal may simply be below the noise floor of a 2-5 m DTM.
    Test this on terrestrial data before committing (that is why P1 comes
    first).
R3  Confusion with rilles, wrinkle ridges, graben and secondary crater chains
    is the dominant failure mode for any linear-depression method.
R4  Shape-from-shading confuses albedo variation with topography. Lunar
    albedo varies with maturity and composition. SfS artefacts can manufacture
    fake depressions.
R5  GRAIL cannot resolve a 60-300 m tube. Do not let a reviewer catch you
    implying otherwise. GRAIL constrains only kilometre-scale mass deficits.
R6  Thermal IR sees the pit, not the cave. Horvath et al. established this
    explicitly. Use Diviner as a pit confirmer, not a void detector.
R7  Compute. Global NAC processing is not a small undertaking. Scope
    regionally and say so up front.
R8  Prior-art collision. Watson & Baldini (2025) already occupy the obvious
    ML-detection niche. Cite them early and position against them clearly.

================================================================================
BOTTOM LINE

The pipeline transfers. The physics does not. Your point cloud gives you the
roof, never the void. Build an inference system, not a detector; anchor it in
terrestrial analogs where ground truth actually exists; fuse the geophysics;
and quantify uncertainty honestly. Do that and you have two to three solid
papers and a result that a real mission can use. Skip the terrestrial anchor
and you have a pretty map that nobody can check.
