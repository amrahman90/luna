=====================================================================
0. WHY THESE TWO PAPERS CHANGE THE PLAN
=====================================================================

Previous versions argued by analogy: forestry pipelines "resemble" what
a lunar void-inference pipeline would need. These two papers replace
analogy with INHERITANCE. They contain three components that solve
problems v3.0 had flagged as open risks, and they were produced by the
same institute, on the same software stack, with published parameters.

THE THREE DECISIVE TRANSFERS

(1) THE ICP + KRIGING DISTORTION MAP [P1] solves the systematic-error
    problem that Gate G1 depends on.
    v3.0 risk R2 was: "the roof-sag signal may be below the DTM noise
    floor." That risk was unquantifiable because systematic vertical
    distortion in a DTM was being treated as irreducible. P1 shows it
    is not. Their sequence, measured at 20 independent check points:
        initial      Z bias +4.18 m,  RMSE 4.53 m
        after ICP    Z bias -0.53 m,  RMSE 1.26 m
        after Kriging Z bias -0.02 m, RMSE 0.21 m
    A factor-of-six further reduction AFTER ICP had already converged,
    achieved by sampling 205 points at locations where no true
    elevation change was expected, kriging those residuals into a
    continuous "distortion map," and subtracting it.
    Critically, P1 reports the kriged surface was a smooth,
    low-frequency gradient contrasting with the high-frequency
    variability of the real signal — i.e. the correction removed
    global alignment error WITHOUT smoothing the localized features
    of interest. That is exactly the property a roof-sag search needs,
    because sag is a low-amplitude but spatially coherent signal that
    naive detrending would destroy.

(2) THE VERTICAL COMPLEXITY INDEX [P2] gives a laptop-scale,
    non-deep-learning, point-cloud-native overhang detector.
    v3.0 Gap 4 argued that 2.5D rasters structurally fail at overhangs
    and that point-cloud-native networks were the answer. P2 offers a
    far cheaper answer. VCI = -sum(p_i ln p_i)/ln(n_HB) is the Shannon
    evenness of the vertical point distribution within a voxel column:
    0 when all points fall in one height bin, 1 when spread evenly.
    Over flat lunar mare, a photogrammetric cloud is effectively 2.5D
    and VCI collapses toward 0. At a PIT WALL OR OVERHANG the cloud is
    genuinely 3D within a single column — so VCI spikes precisely
    where rasters fail. The tree-stem case and the pit-wall case are
    the same geometry: a vertical column of returns.
    P2 ran the whole workflow in about 30 seconds per dataset on an
    Intel Core i7-10810U laptop with 16 GB RAM. This independently
    validates the v3.0 Tier-0 infrastructure claim.

(3) THE SNOW DAMPING FORMALISM [P1, Appendix C] converts Diviner from
    a pit confirmer into a roof-thickness constraint.
    P1 computes a damping factor n_f = exp(-S_eff/D) with damping depth
    D = sqrt(2*kappa/omega), kappa = k_eff/(rho*c_p), then a surface
    temperature offset dT_s = (FDD_a - FDD_s)/(N_f + N_t) with
    FDD_s = n_f * FDD_a.
    D = sqrt(2*kappa/omega) is the standard diffusion skin depth — the
    same expression already used in planetary thermal modelling. The
    PHYSICS is therefore not novel; what transfers is the WORKFLOW
    PATTERN: a remotely-sensed thickness map drives a damping factor,
    which produces a predicted surface-temperature-offset map, which is
    then compared against measurement. Run forward, it predicts the
    thermal signature of a given roof thickness. Run inverse, and a
    measured Diviner nighttime anomaly becomes a constraint on roof
    thickness plus void presence.
    This matters because v3.0 had to concede that thermal IR "sees the
    pit, not the cave." That remains true for detection. But an
    anomaly amplitude still carries quantitative information about the
    material column above a void, and P1 supplies a validated,
    published pipeline shape for extracting it.

1. FULL COMPONENT REUSE MATRIX
=====================================================================

--- FROM [P1] SNOW DEPTH / ARCTIC INFRASTRUCTURE -------------------

C1  Multi-epoch DSM differencing
    Lunar: DTM minus regional trend surface; and sink-filled DTM minus
    DTM (depression-depth raster). Sign inverted — we want negative
    residuals. -> WP2

C2  ICP fine registration (CloudCompare 2.13.2)
    Published parameters directly portable: RMS convergence threshold
    1.0e-7; final overlap 90%; adjust scale enabled; random sampling
    limit 500,000 points; farthest point removal enabled; scalar field
    weights applied to both clouds; no constraints on rotation or
    translation.
    Lunar: registering overlapping NAC stereo-derived clouds to each
    other and to SLDEM2015/LOLA. Note one change: "adjust scale" should
    be DISABLED for lunar work, because scale is fixed by the camera
    model and SPICE geometry, and allowing it absorbs real relief.
    -> WP0, WP2

C3  *** KRIGING DISTORTION MAP *** (pykrige, spherical variogram,
    2000 x 2000 interpolation grid)
    Lunar: select "no-change" reference points on flat mare panels with
    no mapped features, at LOLA track crossings, and on independently
    surveyed flat surfaces; extract residuals between the NAC DTM and
    LOLA; krige into a continuous systematic-error surface; subtract.
    Report the kriged surface's spatial frequency content alongside the
    residual map, exactly as P1 did, to demonstrate the correction did
    not eat the signal. -> WP1, WP2 (highest-priority transfer)

C4  Zero-change internal validation surface (snow-cleared roads)
    P1 extracted 1.9 million pixels within 1 m buffers of road
    centrelines where true depth was known to be ~0, and reported the
    full distribution: mean 10.99 cm, median 8.24 cm, SD 21.64 cm,
    10th/90th percentiles -5.72 / 33.17 cm.
    Lunar: the identical construct is flat mare panels with no mapped
    features. The residual distribution there IS THE NOISE FLOOR, and
    it is the direct, quantitative answer to Gate G1 — whether roof sag
    of a given amplitude is detectable at all. This converts v3.0's
    biggest open risk into a measurement. -> WP1, Gate G1

C5  Watershed segmentation on an inverted DSM (Orfeo ToolBox 8.1.2)
    P1 inverted the DSM so building roofs became catchment basins.
    Parameters: depth threshold 0.01; flood level 0.1; vector mode;
    tile size 4096 px; polygon stitching enabled; minimum object size
    1000 px. Post-processing: area filter 15-4000 m^2; Douglas-Peucker
    simplification at 1 m tolerance.
    Lunar: DO NOT INVERT. Pits and sag are already basins. The same
    algorithm and post-processing chain applies directly, with the area
    filter rescaled to lunar feature sizes (pit funnels roughly
    60-150 m across; sag features 60-300 m wide). -> WP2

C6  Segment-and-buffer statistical extraction with DIRECTIONAL SECTORS
    P1 buffered roads (4 m exclusion + 5 m analysis), segmented into
    max 50 m / min 12 m sections, and classified each into N/S/E/W
    sectors, finding South-facing embankments significantly deeper
    (0.77 m) than North (0.66 m), East (0.60 m) and West (0.60 m) by
    Mann-Whitney U with Bonferroni correction.
    Lunar: buffer and segment along rille centrelines and candidate
    lineaments, then stratify residual depth BY SUN-AZIMUTH SECTOR.
    This is a decisive artifact test: if apparent depression depth
    varies systematically with illumination azimuth, it is a
    shape-from-shading or shadow artifact, not topography. If it does
    not, the feature is real. v3.0 listed SfS albedo confusion as risk
    R4 with no concrete test; this is the test. -> WP2

C7  Reference-surface bias -> conservative lower-bound framing
    P1's snow-free DSM captured shrub canopy tops rather than ground,
    producing systematic underestimation (bias -17.32 cm, RMSE
    30.45 cm, R^2 0.35; improving to -6.7 cm, 20.7 cm, R^2 0.46 with
    high-shrub sites removed). Rather than treat this as failure, P1
    reframed its product as a defensible MINIMUM snow depth.
    Lunar: regolith mantles and smooths the true structural roof
    surface, so any measured sag is a LOWER BOUND on true flexure.
    Adopt the same framing verbatim. This is scientifically honest and,
    for hazard and mission-planning purposes, the conservative
    direction. -> WP2, WP4

C8  Steady-state damping model (Appendix C)
    See transfer (3) above. -> WP4

C9  Honest coverage-bound reporting
    P1 discarded the western part of the study area outright after
    encountering vertical GNSS errors exceeding 60 m that could not be
    corrected, reducing the area to 0.98 km^2 and excluding 14 of 44
    in-situ points. The paper states this plainly in Methods.
    Lunar: the same discipline for NAC DTMs failing the shapefile error
    thresholds, and for the ~2% stereo coverage limit. -> all WPs

C10 Variable-geometry acquisition design (Mueller et al. 2023 spiral
    flight, gimbal continuously varying 0-35 deg off-nadir)
    Lunar: the case for prioritising OFF-NADIR / OBLIQUE NAC
    observations over nadir-only stacks when reconstructing subtle
    topography and, especially, pit walls. -> WP2, WP4

--- FROM [P2] VCI TREE STEM DETECTION -------------------------------

C11 *** VERTICAL COMPLEXITY INDEX as an overhang/verticality detector
    See transfer (2). Implementation: compute VCI per voxel column ->
    raster -> threshold -> local maximum filter (P2 used neighbourhood
    size 5) -> centroid points.
    Lunar parameter translation from P2's calibrated set:
        height segment 3-15 m  ->  depth band below local rim, scaled
                                   to pit depth (e.g. 5-60 m)
        pixel size 0.5 m       ->  2-5 m (NAC DTM posting)
        height bin height 1 m  ->  2-5 m
        VCI threshold 0.4      ->  MUST BE RE-CALIBRATED (see C13)
    -> WP2, WP4

C12 Calibrate-once-then-transfer-unchanged protocol
    P2 calibrated four parameters on a single dataset (SfM22), then
    applied that configuration WITHOUT adjustment to every other
    dataset to test robustness. Best configuration: 3-15 m segment,
    0.5 m pixel, 1 m HBH, 0.4 threshold -> 75.25% of overstory
    reference trees detected, precision 0.8172, F1 0.7835.
    Lunar: calibrate on one well-characterised site (Mare
    Tranquillitatis Pit), then transfer unchanged to Marius Hills and
    Ingenii. Report the unchanged-transfer result as the honest
    headline number. -> WP2, evaluation protocol

C13 *** THRESHOLDS DO NOT TRANSFER ACROSS INSTRUMENTS ***
    The most operationally important finding in P2. Applying the
    SfM-tuned parameter set to the Jülich UAV-LiDAR cloud gave recall
    0.9727 but precision 0.5297 (95 false positives), F1 0.6859.
    Re-tuning a single parameter — the VCI threshold, from 0.4 to 0.8 —
    produced F1 0.86 with recall 0.89 and precision 0.82, exceeding
    every SfM result.
    Lunar consequence: the degradation ladder in WP1 must re-tune the
    threshold AT EVERY RESOLUTION RUNG and every instrument, and report
    threshold sensitivity as a RESULT rather than a nuisance parameter.
    A single fixed threshold applied across GSDs would have
    manufactured a false detectability cliff. -> WP1 (design change)

C14 Full sensitivity heatmap as a deliverable
    P2 reported 96 parameter combinations (2 height segments x 4 pixel
    sizes x 4 HBH x 3 thresholds) as heatmaps, F1 ranging 0.6971 to
    0.7835 — a spread of only ~0.09, which is itself the robustness
    evidence.
    Lunar: this is the exact template for the LLTB-1 ablation tables.
    -> WP1

C15 Declared matching radius with justification
    P2 matched detections to references within 1.5 m and explicitly
    noted there is no community standard, with many studies using wider
    radii — then justified 1.5 m against mean DBH (49.85 cm) and raster
    resolution.
    Lunar: Lunar Pit Atlas coordinates are accurate to roughly 30 m, so
    the matching radius must be at least that, stated up front, with
    sensitivity to the choice reported. -> evaluation protocol

C16 *** APPARENT FALSE POSITIVES MAY BE UNLABELLED TRUE POSITIVES ***
    P2 found 34 FPs against overstory references on SfM22; 21 matched
    documented UNDERSTORY trees, leaving only 13 genuine FPs and
    lifting precision above 0.93 (above 0.87 across all SfM datasets).
    Lunar consequence, and a direct correction to v3.0: with roughly 20
    labelled positives and no verified negatives, EVERY apparent false
    positive must be manually inspected in NAC imagery before being
    scored as an error. Published deep-learning work already
    demonstrated this by surfacing two previously uncatalogued
    skylights while surveying only about 1.9% of the lunar maria.
    Scoring blind against the Pit Atlas systematically understates
    precision. -> evaluation protocol (mandatory change)

C17 Error stratification by object property, with significance testing
    P2 stratified FN vs TP by DBH using Wilcoxon rank-sum (alpha 0.01),
    finding trees below 0.25 m DBH particularly hard to detect, roughly
    81% detected at DBH >= 0.2 m rising to about 91% at DBH >= 0.6 m.
    Lunar: this IS a detectability curve, and it is the structural
    template for WP1's headline product — detection rate stratified by
    pit diameter, sag amplitude, and roof span, with significance
    tests. -> WP1

C18 Independent covariate to control confounds
    P2 computed the Laser Penetration Index from an INDEPENDENT
    airborne laser scanning dataset specifically so the density metric
    would not share data gaps with the SfM cloud under test, and added
    distance-to-nearest-canopy-gap as a second check. LPI differences
    were significant (alpha 0.01); canopy-gap distance was not
    (alpha 0.05) — and P2 reported both, including the null.
    Lunar: test detection performance against covariates derived from a
    DIFFERENT dataset than the one being evaluated — LOLA track
    density, NAC image count per tile, slope from SLDEM2015 — and
    report nulls. -> evaluation protocol

C19 Acquisition-condition effect, honestly hedged
    P2 observed that the 2025 SfM cloud (492 pts/m^2, versus 289-385
    for other years) was acquired under clear skies while all others
    were overcast, and detected substantially more stems (recall
    0.8168, the highest of the SfM set) — then explicitly labelled this
    "a hypothesis based on a single observation" requiring further
    quantitative investigation.
    Lunar: sun incidence angle is the exact equivalent of weather. Copy
    both the analysis and the hedging. -> WP1, WP2

C20 Predictable geometric failure mode
    P2 detected only 4-6 of 11 documented leaning stems, because a
    leaning trunk splits across multiple voxel columns, flattening the
    vertical distribution and lowering VCI.
    Lunar: FUNNEL-SHAPED pit geometry will degrade VCI in exactly the
    same way. Mare Tranquillitatis Pit has a funnel plus a narrower
    inner pit; Marius Hills Hole sits in friable, vesicular material
    with sloped walls. Predict up front that VCI will perform best on
    vertical inner walls and worst on funnel slopes — then test it.
    A pre-registered, mechanistically-derived failure prediction is
    strong methodology. -> WP2, WP4

C21 Height normalisation against an independent ground reference
    P2 normalised point clouds using ground classification from open
    federal geodata rather than from the cloud itself.
    Lunar: normalise NAC DTMs against SLDEM2015 rather than against a
    surface fitted to the NAC DTM itself, avoiding circularity. -> WP2

C22 Demonstrated compute footprint
    ~30 s per dataset on a 16 GB laptop with integrated graphics.
    Lunar: confirms Tier 0 ($10-20/month) suffices for WP0-WP2.
    -> infrastructure

=====================================================================
2. WHAT v3.0 GOT WRONG, CORRECTED
=====================================================================

E1  v3.0 treated DTM systematic error as an irreducible noise floor.
    P1 shows it is reducible by roughly a factor of six after ICP has
    already converged (RMSE 1.26 m -> 0.21 m). Gate G1's pass criterion
    must therefore be evaluated AFTER kriging correction, not before.
    Applying it before would very likely have failed the gate and
    killed a viable project.

E2  v3.0 jumped from raster methods to point-cloud deep learning,
    skipping an entire intermediate class. VCI is interpretable, needs
    no labels, has four parameters, runs in seconds, and attacks
    precisely the overhang case that motivated the deep-learning
    argument. Deep learning moves later and must beat VCI as its
    baseline.

E3  v3.0's evaluation protocol scored candidates against the Lunar Pit
    Atlas. Per C16 that systematically understates precision, because
    unlabelled real features are counted as errors. Manual inspection
    of every apparent FP is now mandatory.

E4  v3.0's degradation ladder held detector parameters fixed across
    resolution rungs. Per C13 that would have produced an artificial
    detectability cliff. Re-tune per rung; report the threshold
    trajectory.

E5  v3.0 listed SfS albedo/topography confusion as a risk without a
    test. C6's sun-azimuth sector stratification is a concrete,
    published-precedent test.

4. REVISED EVALUATION PROTOCOL
=====================================================================

Retained from v3.0: splits by physical pit, site, illumination and
instrument, never by tile; one mare basin held out untouched; false
positives per 10^4 km^2 rather than false-positive rate; conformal
coverage; cross-body testing; the ~0.8%-precision base-rate reality
(~240,000 5 km tiles across the maria against ~20 usable positives).

Added from the two papers:
  - Every apparent false positive manually inspected in NAC imagery
    before scoring (C16), with a separate "unlabelled candidate" class
    reported alongside true errors.
  - Matching radius declared at >= 30 m to respect Pit Atlas positional
    accuracy, with sensitivity to that choice reported (C15).
  - Detection stratified by feature size with Wilcoxon/Kruskal-Wallis
    significance testing (C17).
  - Confound covariates drawn from independent datasets, with nulls
    reported (C18).
  - Threshold sensitivity reported as a result, per instrument and per
    resolution (C13).
  - Zero-change panel residual distributions reported in full —
    mean, median, SD, 10th and 90th percentiles — as P1 did (C4).

=====================================================================
5. STRATEGIC POSITIONING
=====================================================================

The strongest available framing is no longer "we will adapt forestry
methods to the Moon." It is:

  "The DLR Institute of Data Science has published and validated a
   consumer-grade photogrammetric pipeline for detecting sub-metre
   topographic signals beneath obscuring surface layers, including
   quantified systematic-error correction and a voxel-based detector
   for vertical structure. This project transfers that validated
   pipeline to lunar orbital data to search for intact lava tube
   roofs."

That is defensible because it is true, both papers are open access with
open code and data (P1: gitlab.com/dlr-dw/uav-snow-depth-monitoring,
Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.14608566, CC BY 4.0), and the internal
collaboration path to the DLR Institute of Planetary Research is
obvious. It also converts the terrestrial analog campaign from an
expense into an in-house capability.

Two further alignments worth noting. First, the "obscuring layer"
framing is genuinely shared: P1's shrub canopy hides the ground and
forces a minimum-depth interpretation, exactly as lunar regolith
mantles a flexed roof and forces a lower-bound interpretation. Second,
P2's leaf-off acquisition requirement — data collectable only in a
specific seasonal window — maps onto the lunar constraint that pit
interiors are illuminated only near local noon at low latitudes.

=====================================================================
6. BOTTOM LINE
=====================================================================

v3.0 was a defensible plan built on analogy. v4.0 is built on
inheritance: three components with published parameters, measured error
budgets and open code, each solving a problem the earlier plan had
listed as an open risk.

The kriging distortion map turns "is the signal above the noise floor?"
from a gamble into a measurement. The VCI turns overhang detection from
a deep-learning aspiration into a thirty-second computation. The
damping-depth workflow turns Diviner from a pit confirmer into a
roof-thickness constraint.

The physics still does not transfer. The point cloud still gives the
roof and never the void. But the machinery for pulling a low-amplitude,
spatially coherent signal out from under an obscuring layer, and for
proving honestly that you did not invent it, now transfers almost
component for component.
